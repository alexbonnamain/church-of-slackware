KISS is an acronym for "Keep it simple, stupid", meaning exactly what it says. It is a principle widely used by developers that values simplicity, that is, the removal of every layer of complexity in a system to make it leaner, simpler, and more elegant. Slackware is known for being a system whose development and configuration are very faithful to this principle.

Expert-friendly. Patrick Volkerding himself said in an interview that he tries to make Slackware the most expert-friendly distribution, meaning the one that best combines advanced and user-friendly features. He probably said this because there are many advanced distributions, but not many of them are particularly user-friendly.

As for Slackware being Unix-like, this means that it closely follows Unix principles. In other words, the way directories, services, and other components are organized is very faithful to Unix. The advantage is that there is a logical consistency in the overall structure. More information at:

Unix-like - linfo.org
